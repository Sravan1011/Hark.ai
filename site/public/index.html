Why this is hard on Linux — and how Hark does it

Wayland deliberately stops apps from watching keys or typing into other windows. That's why the speech model is the easy part. Hark reads your keyboard directly through the kernel (evdev), so holding and releasing a key works on every compositor, and pastes through a virtual keyboard (uinput) that your desktop treats like real hardware.

The trade-off: a one-time permission (the input group), which the installer sets up and explains. Support for the newer desktop portals is on the roadmap.

Install

One command. Run it as your normal user — it asks for your password only for the system parts.

$ curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Sravan1011/Hark.ai/main/install.sh | bash

What the installer does

  • Installs PortAudio and clipboard tools with your package manager
  • Adds you to the input group so Hark can see your shortcut
  • Allows the virtual keyboard used to paste (/dev/uinput)
  • Installs Hark in its own folder: ~/.local/share/hark
  • Adds hark to your terminal and Hark to your app menu
  • Downloads the default speech model (~670 MB, once)

Requirements

  • Linux with apt, dnf, pacman or zypper
  • Python 3.10+ (already on most distros)
  • ~1.5 GB disk for the app and one model
  • Any microphone. No GPU needed.

Options

… | bash -s -- --no-model    # skip the model download
… | bash -s -- --no-system   # no sudo steps
… | bash -s -- --uninstall   # remove Hark

Tested on Debian 12 with GNOME on Wayland. Other distributions and desktops are supported by design — tell us how it goes.

What leaves your computer?

Nothing you say. Speech is recognized locally, cleaned up locally, and pasted locally.

  • On your computer Your voice, transcripts, history, notes, dictionary and settings
  • On your computer AI cleanup — Ollama runs locally too, if you turn it on
  • Network, once Speech models download from Hugging Face when you choose them
  • Never Accounts, analytics, telemetry or cloud transcription

Questions

Why does Hark need the input group?

Wayland doesn't let apps register a global “hold this key” shortcut. Reading the keyboard through the kernel is the only way that works on every desktop. Membership in input lets programs running as you read keyboard events — the installer asks before adding it. Don't want that? Bind hark toggle to a shortcut in your desktop's settings instead (press to start, press to stop); it needs no special permission.

Does it work on Wayland? GNOME? KDE? Hyprland?

Yes — that's the point. Hark works below the compositor (through the kernel's input devices), so it's designed to behave the same on GNOME, KDE Plasma, Hyprland, Sway, i3 and others, on Wayland or X11. It's been tested most on GNOME with Wayland; if something's off on your desktop, please open an issue.

Do I need a GPU?

No. The default Parakeet model is fast on an ordinary laptop CPU. The largest Whisper model is more accurate but slow without a GPU.

Which languages does it understand?

Parakeet v3 handles 25 European languages; Parakeet v2 is English-only and very accurate. Whisper models cover 99 languages, including Hindi, Telugu, Tamil, Arabic, Chinese and Japanese, with accuracy that varies by language. Voice commands are in English.

The words come out wrong. What can I do?

It's almost always the microphone signal. Many laptops ship with the input volume at maximum, which drowns your voice in noise. Run hark debug-mic --compare: it reports clipping and noise and compares every downloaded model on the same recording. Lowering Settings → Sound → Input to around 30% often fixes it.

How do I uninstall it?

Run the install command with bash -s -- --uninstall. Your models, history and notes are kept unless you add --purge.

Is it really free?

Yes. Hark is open source under the Apache 2.0 license. There's no paid tier and nothing to sign up for.
